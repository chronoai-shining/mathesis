Referee assessmentC1 is well posed and matches an existing conjecture. I classify it as open in the inspected literature—not as a new conjecture, a proved theorem, or a refuted statement. The exact earlier formulation is Conjecture 3.1, equation (37), in On the Efficiency of Random Permutation for ADMM and Coordinate Descent [opened: arXiv:1503.06387]. arXivNo PDF attachment was available in this conversation. This report therefore concerns the complete inline statement and supplied diagnostic excerpts; it does not certify any unseen manuscript.1. Definitions and quantifiersThe intended assertion is
$$\begin{gathered}
n,N\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},\qquad
P_i\in\mathbb R^{N\times N},\qquad
P_i^{\mathsf T}=P_i,\quad P_i^2=P_i,\\
M=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nP_i,\qquad
S=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_n}
P_{\sigma(1)}\cdots P_{\sigma(n)},\\
M^n-S\succeq0.
\end{gathered}$$
Here  $\succeq0$  means
$$x^{\mathsf T}(M^n-S)x\ge0
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^N.$$
The matrices are orthogonal projections. There is no assumption of mutual commutativity, distinctness, fixed rank, or any relation between  $n$  and  $N$ . In particular, repeated matrices and the zero and identity projections are allowed. The  $n!$  summands count permutations of labels, even when some matrices coincide.Although individual products need not be symmetric, their full permutation average is symmetric: transposition reverses the order, and reversal permutes the summands. Thus the positive-semidefinite comparison is meaningful. The only editorial clarification needed is to declare the dimension domain explicitly. Allowing  $N=0$  under a zero-dimensional matrix convention adds only a vacuous case;  $n=0$  is excluded because the mean is undefined.2. Prior formulation and established baselineThe identification with the earlier conjecture requires only expanding  $M,S$  and reversing the permutation order. It does not require adding hypotheses from the surrounding coordinate-descent application. That source separately establishes the weaker inequality
$$S\preceq M$$
as Lemma 3, equation (52). arXiv+1This distinction remains visible in the later Provable Benefit of Random Permutations over Uniform Sampling in Stochastic Coordinate Descent [opened: arXiv:2505.23152]: Appendix E.2 explicitly contrasts the weak bound with the expression containing the  $n$ -th power. The inspected discussion does not supply a general resolution of C1. arXivThe elementary and commutative boundaries should also be acknowledged. The  $n=1$  case is equality by definition. The two-matrix case is covered by the established matrix-order inequality in §3.1 of Toward a Noncommutative Arithmetic-geometric Mean Inequality: Conjectures, Case-studies, and Consequences [opened: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v23/recht12/recht12.pdf]. Its Appendix A supplies the simultaneous-diagonalization and scalar-mean baseline for mutually commuting positive-semidefinite matrices. These cover restrictions of C1, not its unrestricted noncommuting domain. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research+1The remaining obligation is precise. Projection hypotheses give  $0\preceq M\preceq I$ , hence  $M^n\preceq M$ . Consequently, the established bound  $S\preceq M$  cannot simply be strengthened by transitivity to  $S\preceq M^n$ : the proposed right-hand side is smaller. This identifies where binding to the available baseline stops; it is not a proposed proof.3. Why nearby counterexamples do not settle C1Two distinctions are essential.Recht–Ré Noncommutative Arithmetic-Geometric Mean Conjecture is False [opened: arXiv:2006.01510] concerns general positive-semidefinite matrices and norm-based comparisons, including an equivalent normalized scalar-identity bound. Its negative result cannot be transferred to C1 merely by observing that projections are positive semidefinite: a counterexample in a larger class need not belong to the projection subclass. Its three-factor result likewise bounds the symmetrized product between scalar multiples of the identity, rather than by the actual matrix  $M^3$ . arXiv+1Random Reshuffling is Not Always Better [opened: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/42299f06ee419aa5d9d07798b56779e2-Paper.pdf] does include a projection-based counterexample in §2.3. However, that example refutes a comparison of averages of individual product norms. It is not presented there as a refutation of the matrix-order assertion C1. Moving a norm inside or outside an average changes the assertion. NeurIPS ProceedingsMore generally, a norm estimate gives a scalar bound on spectral magnitude; C1 asks for a directional quadratic-form bound using  $M^n$ . Conversely, a one-sided matrix-order bound alone does not control negative eigenvalues sufficiently to establish a two-sided norm assertion.I located no matching proof or refutation of C1 in this bounded search.
