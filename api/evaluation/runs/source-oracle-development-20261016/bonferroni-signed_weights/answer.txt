A listing is a function
$$g:A\times A\longrightarrow Y,$$
with column-dependent product weight
$$w_q(g)=\prod_{a\in A}\prod_{b\in A}q\bigl(b,g(a,b)\bigr).$$
Its twisted diagonal is the function
$$d_{f,g}(b)=f(g(b,b)).$$
A row  $a$  is captured when  $g(a,b)=d_{f,g}(b)$  for every  $b\in A$ , and  $C_f(g)$  counts such rows. This is the matrix notation for the diagonal/off-row coordinates in the supplied Sample, listing, and sampleWeight definitions.The literal assertion quantifies universally over finite  $A,Y$ , every real-valued  $q:A\times Y\to\mathbb R$ , and every  $f:Y\to Y$ . Empty sums and products retain their ordinary conventions, so empty index sets do not make the statement undefined.Two presentation issues should be corrected.
