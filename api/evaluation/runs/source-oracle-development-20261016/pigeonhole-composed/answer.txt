$$\begin{gathered}
\text{For every finite }A,B,C\text{ with }|C|\le |B|<|A|,\\
\forall f:A\to B,\ \forall g:B\to C,\quad
\exists a,a'\in A:\quad
a\ne a'\ \land\ g(f(a))=g(f(a')).
\end{gathered}$$
Here the arrows denote total functions. The pair is existential and may depend on the maps; the statement does not say that every distinct pair has equal images. Writing “there exist distinct  $a,a'\in A$ ” would remove the only appreciable ambiguity.No additional nonemptiness assumption is needed. Choices of sets for which one of the required functions does not exist are vacuous cases of the universal formulation, not counterexamples.
