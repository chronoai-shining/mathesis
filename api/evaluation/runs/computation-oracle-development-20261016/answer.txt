$$M=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_i,\qquad
S=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_n}
P_{\sigma(1)}\cdots P_{\sigma(n)}.$$
The hypotheses are
$$P_i\in\mathbb R^{N\times N},\qquad
P_i^{T}=P_i,\qquad P_i^{2}=P_i.$$
These describe orthogonal projection matrices; they do not require the projections to be mutually orthogonal or to commute.Although an individual ordered product need not be symmetric,  $S$  is symmetric: transposition reverses each product, and reversal permutes the summands. Consequently, the claimed positive semidefiniteness has its ordinary meaning:
$$x^{T}(M^{n}-S)x\geq0
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^{N}.$$
The statement should specify that  $n$  and  $N$  are integers and whether  $N=0$  is allowed. Allowing the zero-dimensional case merely adds a vacuous case; it does not affect the negative finding. The condition  $n\geq1$  makes both averages defined.
