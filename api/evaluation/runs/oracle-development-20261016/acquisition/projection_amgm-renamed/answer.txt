This assessment concerns the confirmed inline statement only; it does not certify the contents of an attachment.Mathematical meaning and hypothesesHere “orthogonal projections” has its standard individual meaning:
$$E,F,G\in M_3(\mathbb R),\qquad
P^{\mathsf T}=P,\quad P^2=P
\quad(P=E,F,G).$$
It does not assert that their ranges are pairwise orthogonal or that the three matrices commute. The statement permits all ranks, including zero and three, and permits repeated projections.For explanatory notation, put
$$M=\frac{E+F+G}{3},\qquad
T=\frac{EFG+EGF+FEG+FGE+GEF+GFE}{6}.$$
C1 asks whether  $M^3-T\succeq0$ , with ordinary matrix multiplication. Both matrices are symmetric: transposition reverses each product in  $T$ , and all reversed products are present. Thus positive semidefiniteness is unambiguous. The fully quantified assertion is
$$\forall E,F,G\text{ as above},\quad
\forall x\in\mathbb R^3,\qquad
x^{\mathsf T}(M^3-T)x\ge0.$$
These are notation and well-posedness checks, not a proof of the inequality.What the opened literature establishesAn exact parent conjecture exists.On the Efficiency of Random Permutation for ADMM and Coordinate Descent [opened: arXiv:1503.06387], §3.3, Conjecture 3.1, equation (37), states the corresponding projection inequality for arbitrary numbers and sizes of matrices. Substituting  $n=N=3$  gives C1. The inspected version leaves that general conjecture unproved. arXiv+1The same work proves the weaker comparison
$$T\preceq M$$
as the three-factor instance of Lemma 3, equation (52). This does not give C1: since  $M$  is a positive contraction,  $M^3\preceq M$ , so the requested upper bound is the smaller matrix. Sharing the upper bound  $M$  does not establish the required comparison between  $T$  and  $M^3$ . arXivThe proved cubic norm result is different.Recht–Ré Noncommutative Arithmetic-Geometric Mean Conjecture is False [opened: arXiv:2006.01510], Theorem 2, equation (4), proves bounds between  $-I$  and  $I$  for the distinct-index cubic average of symmetric positive semidefinite matrices whose sum is at most  $nI$ . Scalar normalization gives the corresponding operator-norm bound. An upper bound by  $\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^3I$ , however, is not an upper bound by the actual matrix  $M^3$ . Neither this theorem nor the paper’s title supplies a refutation of C1. arXivAnother three-factor theorem places the norm inside the sum.An arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for products of three matrices [opened: arXiv:1411.0333], Theorem 1.4, compares averages of individual product norms, for any unitarily invariant norm. It does not assert positivity of the matrix difference in C1. The location of the norm is mathematically substantive. arXivThe commuting subcase has a classical baseline.Toward a Noncommutative Arithmetic-geometric Mean Inequality: Conjectures, Case-studies, and Consequences [opened: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v23/recht12/recht12.pdf], Appendix A, supplies simultaneous diagonalization and the scalar arithmetic–geometric-mean comparison for commuting positive semidefinite matrices. These cover the commuting restriction of C1, not its unrestricted projection domain.
