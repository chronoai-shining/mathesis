This report therefore concerns the quoted C1 and confirmed metadata, not an unseen manuscript or proof.1. Definitions, quantifiers, and meaningThe intended statement should explicitly read:
$$\begin{gathered}
n,N\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 1},\qquad
P_i\in\mathbb R^{N\times N},\qquad
P_i^\top=P_i,\quad P_i^2=P_i\quad(1\le i\le n),\\
M=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_i,\qquad
S=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_n}
P_{\sigma(1)}P_{\sigma(2)}\cdots P_{\sigma(n)},\\
M^n-S\succeq0.
\end{gathered}$$
Here  $\mathfrak S_n$  consists of permutations of the labels, so equal matrices still contribute with permutation multiplicity. Products and powers are ordinary matrix products. Positive semidefiniteness means
$$x^\top(M^n-S)x\ge0
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^N.$$
The assumptions specify orthogonal projections. No commutativity, rank restriction, distinctness, or nonsingularity is stated or needed to define the question. In particular, zero and identity projections are allowed.There is no hidden symmetry defect: transposition reverses each product, and reversal permutes the summands defining  $S$ . Thus  $S$ , and consequently  $M^n-S$ , is symmetric. This is a well-posedness check, not an argument establishing the inequality.2. Exact antecedent and relevant known resultsExact prior conjecture. Sun, Luo, and Ye’s On the Efficiency of Random Permutation for ADMM and Coordinate Descent [opened: arXiv:1503.06387], version 3, §3.3, Conjecture 3.1, equation (37), states C1. Their reversed factor order gives the same permutation average. arXivA weaker proved bound. The same work’s Lemma 3, equation (52), establishes
$$S\preceq M$$
for projection matrices; its proof is in §7.3. This does not supply the requested upper bound  $M^n$ . arXivEstablished special cases. Toward a Noncommutative Arithmetic-geometric Mean Inequality: Conjectures, Case-studies, and Consequences [opened: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v23/recht12/recht12.pdf] gives the two-matrix positive-semidefinite-order inequality in §3.1. Its Appendix A records the simultaneous-diagonalization and scalar-inequality baseline for commuting positive semidefinite matrices. Consequently,  $n=2$  and the pairwise-commuting restriction are known special cases, not evidence that the unrestricted question is settled. The  $n=1$  case is immediate from the submitted definitions.
